# Guideline comparison provenance and maintenance

The independent collection in `data/headache_guidelines.json` is an editorial comparison of official guidance. It is **not** generated by the daily PubMed/AI pipeline and is not subject to the paper collection's JIF or publication-window rules. Its review date must never be refreshed merely because the paper sync ran.

Reviewed **2026-10-18**. Coverage: Taiwan, Japan, nine European countries (United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Denmark, Switzerland, Finland), plus EHF/EAN. NICE and Scottish SIGN are separate jurisdictions. This is not an exhaustive inventory of Europe or of every ICHD syndrome. Migraine has the broadest coverage; cluster headache and trigeminal neuralgia contain only individually verified comparisons. An absent jurisdiction-topic pair is a coverage gap, not a claim that the country lacks guidance.

## Version checks

Check the current official index **and** the document's own publication/version information. Website upload dates, search-engine dates, surveillance announcements and planned revisions are not new clinical guidelines.

- Taiwan: [THS](https://taiwanheadache.org.tw/) and original publications [acute 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42723131/), [prevention 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36089629/). These have different scopes; the acute update does not supersede preventive rankings. The preventive full text has now been reviewed (official THS PDF linked in the dataset), especially printed pages 198–202. Acute 2026 extraction remains limited to the original abstract; its unverified grades are not borrowed from 2022.
- Japan: [JHS index](https://www.jhsnet.net/guideline.html), comprehensive 2021 guideline and 2025-10-27 CGRP position statement. Keep the first-line clinical position separate from the optimal-use criteria quoted in the statement.
- UK: [NICE CG150](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg150) (core last updated 2025-06-03; technology-appraisal links can be newer); [SIGN/NHS toolkit](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/pharmacological-management-of-migraine-sign/) explicitly identifies the 2026 update. A planned NICE prevention revision is not an implemented recommendation.
- Germany: [DMKG guidelines](https://www.dmkg.de/leitlinien/), DGN S1 v7.1, comprehensive revision 2025-08-08 and correction 2025-11-06. The PDF states validity through 2028-08-07.
- France: [SFEMC recommendations](https://sfemc.fr/professionnels/recommandations/) retain 2021 guidance; the **2024** position paper is a partial update, posted by SFEMC on 2025-03-05. Both the 2021 pharmacological guideline and the 2024 author-archived full text have now been reviewed. Website posting/deposit dates are not publication dates.
- Italy: [SISC index](https://www.sisc.it/ita/linee-guida-diagnostico-terapeutiche-delle-cefalee-primarie_33.html), SISC/IHS 2025 joint guideline. The published summary tables were reviewed. Recommendation strength is not treatment-line order or AIFA reimbursement policy.
- Spain: [SEN 2026 manual](https://web.sen.es/profesionales/guias-y-protocolos/3843-manual-de-practica-clinica-en-cefaleas-edicion-2026), fifth edition; migraine acute chapter and preventive algorithm.
- Netherlands: [NVN specialist migraine modules](https://richtlijnendatabase.nl/richtlijn/hoofdpijn/migraine.html); the comparison specifically uses the CGRP-antibody module dated 2023-12-04. It does not claim that other modules or primary-care guidance have the same date.
- Denmark: [NNBV migraine](https://nnbv.dk/migraene-behandling/) updated 2026-09-19; separate CGRP chapter fifth edition 2026-07-28. The comparison does not reproduce every listed drug/indication, and keeps treatment eligibility in a separate column.
- Switzerland: [SKG therapy commission](https://www.headache.ch/portrait/therapiekommission) links **12th edition**, copyright 2026. The older downloads page/search snippets mentioning edition 11.2 are not the current linked edition.
- Finland: [Käypä hoito](https://www.kaypahoito.fi/hoi36050) dated 2024-03-28, marked under revision. Planned 2026 updates are not published guidance.
- Cross-border: EHF 2022 CGRP guideline (first-line positioning is expert consensus, Table 9); EAN cluster 2023 (full text, especially Tables 16–17) and TN 2019 (UCL accepted manuscript and publisher text). These are not substitutes for country-specific recommendations or drug access rules.

## Data contract and review process

Each source records a publisher, scope, version date (year/month/day only as precisely known), document type, stable HTTPS source, review date, reading depth and version caveat. Each comparison is one jurisdiction plus one syndrome. Every summarized domain requires a source ID and locator; `not_covered` means outside that document's scope, while `not_verified` means no current verified comparison is available. Administrative conditions quoted from older documents are explicitly historical.

To update: check the official source and its supersession status, read the relevant recommendation and qualifying text, update only the affected claims/versions, and preserve exact provenance. Never infer a national position from an EHF/EAN paper. Do not treat a drug authorization, funding decision or position statement as a comprehensive guideline. Keep adult summaries concise and avoid adding unsourced doses or extrapolating to pregnancy/children.

The UI displays the review date, warns after 90 days, shows explicit country-topic gaps, and links each claim to its source. It never labels daily paper synchronization as a fresh guideline review. Content uses original concise paraphrases; source PDFs are not redistributed in this repository.

## Validation

`node --test tests/*.test.cjs` validates source references, source URLs, version dates, coverage, treatment/access separation, filter intersections, stale-data notices and the service worker's preservation of verified cached data on invalid network responses. `python -m pytest -q` exercises the existing pipeline without external API calls. Manual browser verification covers mobile layout, independent guideline failure/retry, source expansion, offline data and the existing read/star controls.

## Drug-level recommendation and evidence grades (schema 2)

`recommendations` in each jurisdiction–syndrome comparison holds individual drug or explicitly named drug-group/context entries. There are 111 entries: 61 have both native recommendation wording/grades and evidence ratings, while 12 carry outcome/study-specific evidence profiles. These are not 61 harmonized formal COR/LOE pairs; native wording such as Offer, Consider and Recommended remains labeled by its own system. This is a selection of comparison-relevant drugs, not a complete formulary. A recommendation includes indication/population, treatment domains, regimen where relevant to grading, one original source and locator, review date, grading system, separate COR/LOE objects and explanatory wording/notes.

Both grades use explicit states: `reported` requires an original code or expressly identified native recommendation wording allowed by the cited grading system; `not_reported` means the cited passage did not provide a formal grade, while `not_verified` means extraction/verification is incomplete (for example, only an abstract is available). Both missing states require a null value. A known recommendation strength can coexist with an unknown evidence level. A first-line statement never supplies a missing grade. Source-level allowed grading systems prevent accidental cross-guideline assignment.

Verified anchors:

- **THS 2022**, printed pp. 198–202: native Recommendation Groups I–V and Evidence Grades A–C. Amitriptyline for episodic migraine is Group I / Grade B despite being second-line in the abstract. Flunarizine is Group I / Grade A for episodic and Group II / Grade B for chronic migraine. These differences are preserved, not reconciled by inference. Full-text grouping is not cardiology Class I or treatment-line ordering.
- **JHS 2021**: CQ II-2-3 is the weak/B recommendation comparing triptans with NSAIDs for disabling attacks. CQ II-3-6 (propranolol), II-3-7 (lomerizine), IV-6–8 (cluster) and VIII-20 (TN) give their own strength/certainty. Only II-2-3 explicitly uses the GRADE workflow; the general Japanese strong/weak and A/B/C scheme is labeled separately. The 2025 CGRP first-line position is ungraded here.
- **EHF 2022 Table 2**: keep episodic/chronic, regimen and comparator separate. Erenumab in chronic migraine has High certainty at 70 mg/month and Moderate at 140 mg/month in this version. Its head-to-head comparison with topiramate is Strong / Low. Do not import Table 9 expert-consensus first-line positioning into this GRADE table.
- **SISC/IHS 2025 summary Tables 3, 5, 6**: use the table captions for populations (episodic in Table 5, chronic in Table 6; the narrative cross-reference order is inconsistent). Retain drug doses when required to identify the rating, e.g. ibuprofen oral 200/400/600 mg is Strong in favor / Low in Table 3.
- **SEN 2026**: acute NSAID/triptan recommendations in chapter 3 and onabotulinumtoxinA for chronic migraine in chapter 4 use GR-A / NE-I. GR is the recommendation axis; NE is evidence, not a Class I recommendation.
- **EAN cluster 2023 full text**: oxygen Strong / Very low; SC sumatriptan Strong / Low; verapamil Consensus with no GRADE certainty; episodic-cluster galcanezumab Weak / Low. EAN TN 2019 gives carbamazepine Strong / Moderate and oxcarbazepine Strong / Very low.
- **European full-text audit**: SIGN 2026 study levels, French 2021 population-specific pairs, Finnish A/B evidence summaries, German descriptive evidence and Dutch/NICE outcome-specific ratings are now incorporated. See [the audit report](european-guideline-audit.md) for original sources, findings and remaining limits. Danish/Swiss documents and the French 2024 position retain explicit ungraded states. A guideline development category such as S1 is not a drug class of recommendation. A result-specific GRADE rating is not automatically a single overall drug grade.

Regimens in the table identify the source's grading context; they are not individualized dosing instructions. The UI keeps treatment/access context available and never maps heterogeneous grades into a single color-ranked clinical hierarchy. The service worker uses schema 2 validation before caching. Versioned reader/worker validator URLs prevent stale HTTP cache mixing, and the reader retries when an older controller is replaced during an update. Browser verification includes upgrading an installed schema 1 app, then reading the schema 2 tables offline.

### Outcome-specific evidence (backward-compatible schema 2 extension)

Optional `evidence_profiles` list an `outcome`, native `value` (or null), `source_id`, `locator`, and `reason` (mandatory when ungraded). Every source must belong to the comparison and allow that grading system. Profiles require a null `not_reported` overall evidence object: they cannot silently supply a global rating. The reader displays profiles in the LOE cell with source dates and locations. `evidence_context` clarifies what a single native rating applies to; `grading_review` explains each jurisdiction's audit finding. These optional fields are validated before offline caching. Sources may be older evidence reviews underlying current recommendations; the UI keeps both dates visible. SIGN page-margin levels describe a study/evidence block and must not be presented as GRADE outcome certainty.

The audit preserves unknowns: `not_reported` is limited to the cited passage, not proof that no appendix has any grading. The seven NICE TA entries cite their recommendation and public evidence-discussion chapters; all committee attachments have not been individually extracted. France's 2021 grades are historical evidence, not grades retrospectively assigned to the 2024 first-line position.
